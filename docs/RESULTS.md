# Measured results and interpretation

Canonical results: `artifacts/metrics.json`, generated by `python -m rebound.benchmark`. Primary seed 71. The source includes development changes; this is an exploratory synthetic engineering study, not a preregistered or external test.

## Future sustained low-PEF event

904 eligible issue windows from 24 held-out test patients, with 298 positives. Passive RECAP AUROC 0.9433, Brier 0.06432, precision 91.27%, sensitivity 84.23%, at threshold 0.5. Counts: TP 251, FP 24, FN 47, TN 582. The endpoint includes ongoing low states, so these statistics do not establish warning before attack onset. The 120-minute forecast MAE is 0.07237 of personal best, or 7.24 percentage points of personal best.

## Ablations

The single-recovery-setting bank has AUROC 0.9442 and Brier 0.06330; recovery diversity does not improve event classification here, although PEF forecast MAE is slightly lower for full RECAP (0.07237 versus 0.07477). Removing personal EHR baselines gives AUROC 0.8007 and Brier 0.16493. This supports personalization within this simulator, which deliberately varies personal baselines; it does not validate general patient benefit.

The snapshot fusion classifier has AUROC 0.9487 and Brier 0.05791. Wearable/context-only classifier has AUROC 0.9478 and Brier 0.05736. Both score better than passive RECAP on Brier. This falsifies any claim that the new composition is universally the most accurate classifier. Its proposed value is an inspectable dynamical explanation and observation-selection workflow, not guaranteed predictive superiority.

The RR >22 baseline has AUROC 0.6933, Brier 0.24889, precision 65.27%, sensitivity 52.35%. The threshold is an illustrative comparator, not a clinical alarm threshold recommendation.

## Measurement policies

All policies have regular four-hourly PEF. Fixed extra PEF at 09:00 and 17:00 uses 48 optional observations across 24 patients, Brier 0.06218, AUROC 0.94657. Adaptive selection uses 38 optional observations, Brier 0.05933, AUROC 0.94633. Adaptive used 20.8% fewer optional measurements and slightly improved pooled Brier, but AUROC was slightly lower. Both have a maximum two-probe budget, not equal realized counts.

Paired patient-mean Brier difference, adaptive minus fixed: -0.002823; 1,000-resample patient-bootstrap 95% interval [-0.005359, -0.000164]. Negative favors adaptive. This is evidence for the policy in this one invented population, not real-world burden reduction. A single simulator, chosen burdens and approximate information model can create optimistic or fragile results.

## Shift

12 new synthetic patients with seed 93, slower activity recovery, changed exposure sensitivity and larger PEF noise: 452 eligible windows, 169 positives, AUROC 0.90592 and Brier 0.07505. This shows performance degradation, and is not independent clinical validation.

## What not to claim

Do not say “first-ever asthma twin,” “never-before-invented algorithm,” “clinically accurate,” “two hours before every attack,” “proven sensor-fault diagnosis,” “recovery diversity improves every outcome,” or “better than every baseline.” No data here support those statements. The implemented algorithm and its negative as well as positive experiments are the contribution available for review.
